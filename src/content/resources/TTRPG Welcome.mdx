Welcome, adventurer! Whether you're brand new to tabletop role-playing games or just looking for helpful tools and links, this page will guide you through the basics — and beyond.

---

## What Is a TTRPG?

A tabletop role-playing game (TTRPG) is a collaborative storytelling experience where players take on the roles of characters in a shared world. One person acts as the game master (GM), describing the setting and adjudicating the rules. The rest of the players interact with the world through their characters using dice, character sheets, and imagination.

While Dungeons & Dragons is the most well-known system, it's just the tip of the iceberg — many other systems exist, each with their own flavor, mechanics, and accessibility.

---

## What Do You Need to Play?

At its simplest, a tabletop role-playing game needs only a few things:

- **A game and its rules.** A [[Starter Kits|starter kit]] or free quickstart is often enough for your first session.
- **People to play with.** Most games use one game master and a small group of players, although solo, cooperative and game-master-free systems also exist.
- **Character sheets and something for notes.** Printed sheets, a notebook or digital tools all work.
- **A way to resolve uncertain actions.** This is usually dice, but some games use cards, tokens or no randomizer at all.

Maps, miniatures, music, virtual tabletops and elaborate accessories can be fun, but none of them are required.

## Terms

Like most hobbies, TTRPGs have jargon and terms that can be intimidating at first. I've listed a few here; you can visit the [[Glossary]] to read a more comprehensive list.

- **System** – The set of rules used to play a game. Different systems have different ways to handle combat, movement, skills, and storytelling. Examples: _Dungeons & Dragons_, _Cairn_, _Shadowdark_.
- **Mechanics** – The specific rules that tell you how to do something in the game (for example, how to roll dice to see if you hit a target).
- **Session** – One meeting where the game is played. It could last a couple of hours or a whole day.
- **Campaign** – A series of connected game sessions that tell an ongoing story with the same characters.
- **One-shot** – A short adventure designed to be finished in one session (though sometimes it takes more).
- **Adventure Module** – A prepared story or setting, often published, that a group can play through.

Here are some great choices to start with. Some systems even have specific [[Starter Kits]] to help get you started.

## Modules & Adventures

An adventure module is a prepared scenario containing locations, characters, situations and challenges that a group can play through. A **supplement** is the broader term for additional material such as settings, character options, monsters or optional rules. This ecosystem lets creators build on games and explore ideas well beyond their core books.

- **[DriveThruRPG](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/)** — Adventures, settings, systems and all sorts of independent content can be found here in either PDF form, or printed on demand.
- [Itch.io](https://itch.io/physical-games/tag-ttrpg) — Itch is a more general game/creative marketplace, but a number of TTRPG authors sell/distribute their games and modules there.

## Where to Find Games

My advice here is to bring your existing friends or family together and try playing a single one-shot with them to see if any of them are interested in playing something more regularly. It is usually easier if you are willing to be the game master. Still, even if not, it's often easier to organize a game with your existing social group rather than trying to find another group somewhere else.

It is possible that your existing social group is not interested in playing, in which case finding some other like-minded folks, either in person or online, is the next best option.

- Local libraries and game stores are great places to start; they may have existing drop-in sessions you can attend. However, smaller communities may not have this option either.
- [Discord](https://discord.com) is a social platform hosting various communities, including many communities around TTRPG products. Suppose you see this icon <Icon icon="discord" class="text-discord size-5" /> on a site. You can usually click it to join their Discord community. Many of these communities have channels for people looking for games to play.
- [StartPlaying.games](https://startplaying.games) — Join a game run by a professional GM.

## Session Zero & Safer Play

A **session zero** is a conversation before regular play begins. It is a chance to agree on the game's premise and tone, make characters together, discuss scheduling and table rules, and establish subjects the group does not want included or wants handled off-screen.

- [TTRPG Safety Toolkit](https://ttrpgsafetytoolkit.com/) — A living collection of table-support tools for use before, during and after play.
- [Consent in Gaming](https://www.montecookgames.com/store/product/consent-in-gaming/) — A free guide and checklist for discussing potentially difficult content.

These tools do not replace trust or good communication, but they give a group shared language for addressing discomfort without derailing play.
