Tabletop role-playing games share a lot of vocabulary, but few terms are truly universal. A word may have a more specific meaning in a particular game, and some terms are associated mainly with one family of systems.

## Core Concepts & Publications

- **Tabletop Role-Playing Game (TTRPG)** – A game in which people describe the actions of characters in a shared imagined world, using rules to help decide what happens.
- **System** – The rules and procedures used to play a game. Different systems handle uncertainty, combat, character growth and storytelling in different ways.
- **Setting** – The fictional world, time period or genre in which a game takes place. One system can support many settings, and a setting can sometimes be adapted to multiple systems.
- **Edition** – A published version of a game. A new edition may make anything from small revisions to major rules changes.
- **Core Rulebook** – The primary book containing the rules needed to play or run a game.
- **Supplement / Sourcebook** – Additional material for a game, such as a setting, character options, monsters, adventures or optional rules.
- **System Reference Document (SRD)** – A publisher-provided reference containing rules or other game material that may be read, reused or licensed under stated terms. An SRD is not always the complete game.
- **Quickstart / Test Drive** – A shortened introduction designed to teach a game, often using pre-generated characters and an included adventure.
- **Adventure Module** – A prepared scenario or location containing situations, characters and challenges for a group to play through.
- **Session** – One meeting in which the game is played. It might last a couple of hours or a whole day.
- **Campaign** – A series of connected sessions, usually following an ongoing group of characters or a shared setting.
- **One-shot** – A short adventure intended to finish in one session, although some take longer in practice.
- **Actual Play** – A recording, stream, podcast or performance of people playing a TTRPG.

## People, Characters & Ways to Play

- **Game Master (GM)** – The person who describes the world, portrays its inhabitants and adjudicates the rules. Some games use names such as Dungeon Master (DM), Keeper, Warden, Guide or Referee.
- **Player Character (PC)** – A character primarily controlled by a player.
- **Non-Player Character (NPC)** – A character portrayed by the game master or governed by the game rather than by one of the regular players.
- **Party** – The group of player characters who adventure or pursue goals together.
- **Game-Master-Free / GMless** – A game that distributes the traditional game master's responsibilities among the players or handles them through procedures and prompts.
- **Solo Play** – Playing a TTRPG alone, usually with rules, prompts or random tables that help answer questions and create situations.
- **Cooperative / Co-op Play** – Players share responsibility for the world and story without a dedicated game master.
- **Player versus Player (PvP)** – Direct conflict between player characters. Groups usually benefit from agreeing in advance whether and when it is allowed.
- **In Character (IC)** – Speaking or acting from a character's point of view.
- **Out of Character (OOC)** – Speaking as a player rather than as a character.
- **Table Talk** – Conversation between players outside the fiction, often used to clarify rules, coordinate plans or check in with one another.
- **Session Zero** – A conversation before regular play begins to establish the premise, make characters, align expectations and discuss boundaries.

## Dice & Resolution

- **Die / Dice Notation** – Dice are commonly written as `d` followed by the number of sides. `d20` means one twenty-sided die; `2d6+3` means roll two six-sided dice, add them together and then add three.
- **Roll** – Using dice or another randomizer to help determine an uncertain outcome.
- **Check / Test** – A roll made to find out whether an attempted action succeeds or what it costs.
- **Modifier** – A number added to or subtracted from a roll or game value.
- **Target Number / Difficulty Class (DC)** – A number a roll must meet, exceed or sometimes roll under to succeed.
- **Dice Pool** – A group of dice rolled together, with the result determined by counting successes, keeping certain dice or adding them.
- **Save / Saving Throw** – A roll made to resist, avoid or reduce a harmful effect.
- **Advantage / Disadvantage** – Rolling additional dice and keeping the better or worse result. The exact procedure varies by system.
- **Critical Success / Critical Failure** – An exceptionally good or bad result, usually triggered by a particular die result or degree of success.
- **Initiative** – A rule or procedure that establishes who acts when during combat or another structured scene.

## Characters & Progression

- **Attribute / Ability Score** – A broad measure of a character's capabilities, such as Strength, Dexterity or Intelligence.
- **Stat** – Any numerical or mechanical value describing a character, creature or object.
- **Skill** – A trained or learned capability, such as lockpicking, medicine or persuasion.
- **Class / Playbook** – A package of abilities or narrative themes that helps define a character's role. Many games do not use classes.
- **Subclass** – A more specialized path within a class.
- **Ancestry / Species / Kin** – Terms used by different games for the kind of person or creature a character is.
- **Background** – A character's previous occupation, culture or life experience, sometimes with mechanical benefits.
- **Backstory** – The character's personal history, important relationships and motivations before play begins.
- **Alignment** – A category describing a character's moral outlook, cosmic allegiance or relationship to order and chaos. It is most strongly associated with D&D and related games.
- **Feat / Talent** – A special ability selected during character creation or advancement.
- **Proficiency** – Training that grants access to an action, item or bonus.
- **Hit Points (HP)** – A resource commonly used to track a character's ability to withstand harm. What HP represents varies substantially by game.
- **Hit Die** – In D&D and related games, a die connected to a character's health and recovery.
- **Experience Points (XP)** – Points awarded through play and used to track or purchase advancement.
- **Level** – A step in a character's progression. Many systems instead use individual improvements, milestones or changes within the fiction.
- **Inventory** – The items and equipment a character carries or has available.
- **Loot** – Money, equipment, information or other rewards acquired during play.
- **Condition / Status** – A temporary state such as frightened, stunned, exhausted or poisoned that affects the fiction or rules.

## Adventures & Story Structure

- **Encounter** – A distinct situation or challenge. It may involve combat, negotiation, exploration, a puzzle or another obstacle.
- **Story Hook** – A clue, offer, threat or event that invites the characters into an adventure.
- **Big Bad / BBEG** – Informal terms for a campaign's main villain or greatest threat. BBEG is often expanded as “Big Bad Evil Guy.”
- **MacGuffin / McGuffin** – An item or objective whose importance motivates the characters and moves the plot forward.
- **Dungeon Crawl** – Play focused on exploring a dangerous location room by room, often involving navigation, resource management and discovery.
- **Hexcrawl** – Exploration organized around a map divided into hexagonal areas, with procedures for travel, encounters and discovering locations.
- **Sandbox** – A campaign in which players choose among multiple goals and locations rather than following one predetermined plot.
- **Railroad** – A pejorative term for play in which meaningful choices are repeatedly overridden to force a predetermined outcome. A linear adventure is not necessarily a railroad if players knowingly accept its premise and retain meaningful choices.
- **Theatre of the Mind** – Playing without a precise tactical map, using verbal description and shared imagination to establish positions and action.
- **Battle Map / Grid** – A visual representation used to track relative or exact positions during an encounter.
- **Narrative Control** – The authority to establish facts about the characters, world or outcome of an action.
- **Improvisation (Improv)** – Creating dialogue, characters, situations or world details during play rather than preparing them in advance.
- **Roleplay** – Making decisions from a character's perspective and portraying how they interact with the world. It does not require acting or speaking in a particular voice.
- **Worldbuilding** – Creating the places, cultures, histories, people and relationships that make up a setting.
- **Total Party Kill (TPK)** – A situation in which every player character dies or is otherwise removed from play.

## Rules Styles & Community Terms

- **Rules as Written (RAW)** – An interpretation based closely on the published wording of the rules.
- **Rules as Intended (RAI)** – An interpretation based on what the designers appear to have meant, which may differ from a literal reading.
- **Ruling** – A decision about how to resolve a situation, especially when the written rules are unclear or deliberately leave room for judgment.
- **House Rule** – A change or addition used by a particular group.
- **Homebrew** – Unofficial game material created by players or third-party creators, including rules, settings, classes, creatures and adventures.
- **Rules-Light** – A relative description for games with fewer or less detailed mechanics. It does not necessarily mean the game is easier to run or play.
- **Crunch / Crunchy** – Informal terms for mechanical detail, interacting rules and character-build options.
- **Fiction-First** – An approach in which the group begins with what is happening in the imagined situation and then applies the relevant rule.
- **Old-School Renaissance / Revival (OSR)** – A broad family of games and play styles inspired by early role-playing games, often emphasizing exploration, player problem-solving, risk and rulings.
- **Powered by the Apocalypse (PbtA)** – A family of games inspired by *Apocalypse World*, commonly using fiction-triggered moves and playbooks. PbtA games can differ greatly from one another.
- **Virtual Tabletop (VTT)** – Software that provides a shared digital play space, often including maps, tokens, dice, character sheets or communication tools.
- **Meta-gaming** – Using knowledge held by the player but not their character. Some games discourage it, while others deliberately ask players to make decisions with a broader view of the story.

## Table Safety & Communication

- **Safety / Support Tool** – A procedure that helps participants communicate boundaries, pause play or redirect content before, during or after a game.
- **Line** – Content the group agrees will not appear in the game.
- **Veil** – Content that may exist in the story but will not be described in detail, often handled by fading to black or moving the scene forward.
- **X-Card** – A tool that lets a participant signal that the current content should stop, change or be skipped without requiring an explanation.
- **Open-Door Policy** – An explicit agreement that anyone may step away from or leave a game at any time without being pressured to remain.
- **Check-In** – A brief pause to confirm that participants are comfortable continuing with the current content or intensity.
- **Debrief / Aftercare** – Time after a session to discuss the experience, decompress and check on one another, particularly after emotionally intense play.
